Screen context

It sees what you see. So you never paste.

Nook keeps the text of every window up to date with on‑device OCR, across all your displays. Say "put this in my calendar", or press ⌥⌘N, and it pulls the dates, places and promises out of the window in front.

Sources above every answerWhich windows and services it searched, what came back empty, and numbered citations that open in Mail, Finder or your browser.
Drop anything inImages, screenshots, PDFs and documents, or a screen region with ⌥⌘4.

Plans

Ask for three things. Check them, then Run.

"Make reminders for the open items and draft an email to Lisa." Nook builds one plan. Turn steps on or off, edit the text, and only then press Run. If a step fails, you see what worked and pick up from there.

Small things that add up.

Memory without setup"Remember that Lisa is my wife." It also learns from your corrections. Everything stays in a file on your Mac that you can search, edit or empty.
Think AlongOptional. When you focus on something for a while, Nook can quietly offer help, like adding it to your calendar. Mute it per app.
Nook takes overTap right ⌘ and say what to do: fill in a form, order the coffee filters. A card waits for your yes before anything that pays, sends, deletes or books.
Talk to it⌥Space for on‑device speech, or the local "Hey Nook" wake word, with spoken answers if you like.
Call notesRecords a call on your Mac, tells the speakers apart, and gives you a transcript, a summary and the action items.
Easy on your batteryBackground work slows down on battery and pauses when your Mac is hot, busy or asleep. What you ask for always runs at full speed.

A buddy, not a tool

It has a face. And good timing.

Nook looks at your cursor, nods when a task is done, and wears a ring that shows what's going on: white for working, amber for waiting on you, coral for a problem, mint for done.
