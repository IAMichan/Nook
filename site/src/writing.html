How it works

Suggestions appear as grey text after your cursor

You typeIn Mail, Slack, Notes, a browser, anywhere with a text field. Password fields are always skipped.
Nook reads the fieldThe text around your cursor, the app and the site, and if you allow it, what's on screen and your clipboard.

Context

It knows when to stay out of the way

On your own MacYour built‑in MLX model or any GGUF model through llama.cpp, accelerated by Metal. Nothing you type leaves your Mac.
Per app and per websiteFormal in Mail, short in Slack, Dutch on one site. Turn it off where you don't want it.
Learns how you writeYour words and the phrases you write again and again, learned locally and stored encrypted. Card numbers, keys and passwords are filtered out first.
Emoji, typos, other wordsType : for emoji, get a typo fixed inline, or pick another word by number.
Easy on your batteryPauses on battery by default, or runs lighter: only when you pause typing, shorter, or with a smaller model.
Also in your editor's chatIn the Claude Code or Copilot chat box of VS Code and Cursor, never in the code editor or the terminal.

Statistics show how many words you completed and the time you saved. Nook only counts; it never stores what you typed.

FAQ

Common questions

Does what I type leave my Mac?

No. Autocomplete runs on a model on your Mac, and password fields and Secure Input are never read. Statistics are counts only, never your text.

Which apps does it work in?

Almost every app with a text field, including browsers and Electron apps. Terminals, code editors and password managers are off by default, and you can switch any app on or off in Settings › Autocomplete.

Will Tab still work normally?

Yes. Nook only takes Tab while a suggestion is on screen. With nothing showing, Tab belongs to the app, and you can leave Tab to an app entirely.

Can I try it before turning it on everywhere?

Yes. Settings › Autocomplete has a Playground where you can type and see the suggestion, how long it took, and the exact prompt the model read.

Does dictation need the internet?

No. It runs on‑device, or offline with Parakeet on the Neural Engine.
