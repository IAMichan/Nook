Approvals

One card for every action. One log you can read back.

A reply to a friend, an event in your calendar, a command from a coding agent or a pull request: it all goes through the same approval card, shown in full, and lands in a local audit log.

What Nook leaves alone

Some things it never reads. Some things it never keeps.

Password fieldsSecure fields and Secure Input are skipped by screen reading, autocomplete and Nook taking over. Password, payment and identity fields always stay empty.
Secrets in textCard numbers, IBANs, ID numbers, API keys, tokens and private keys are filtered from screen text and the clipboard before a model sees them.
What you typedAutocomplete statistics are counts only: how many words, how often accepted. Never the words themselves.
Your writing samplesWhat autocomplete learns from your writing is encrypted on disk, with a key in Nook's Keychain vault.
Agents' reachAgents get their own secrets only, never Nook's keys. They start tracked commands and cannot type into your shell. Nook's local socket answers only your own user.
The agents' browserNo file:// or custom links. Each project keeps its own sign‑ins, and Nook asks before it keeps one.
Energy

Quiet on your battery, too.

Nook works in the background so answers are ready at once. One policy slows that down on battery and pauses it when the battery runs low, your Mac is hot or busy, or you are away. What you ask for always runs at full speed.

Does Nook send my screen to the cloud?

Not by default. The assistant runs on your Mac and reads your screen there. If you switch to a cloud model, you choose who reads the screen: that model, nobody, or a model on your Mac. Mark the screen as Only on this Mac and a cloud model never sees it.

Do I need an account or an API key?

No. Nook works out of the box with a model on your Mac. Cloud keys are optional and stored in your Mac's Keychain.

Can a coding agent do something I didn't approve?

Read‑only work runs on its own. Anything else waits for your Allow, unless you gave a session "Allow everything". Dangerous commands are refused either way, and every decision is written to the audit log.

Where is my data stored?

On your Mac: memories, chats with Nook and the audit log live in a local database you can inspect and delete in Settings. Keys and tokens are kept in the Keychain.

Can Nook be tricked by text on a web page or in an email?

That kind of prompt injection is exactly why every action needs your yes. Reports about it are especially welcome through the security policy.
