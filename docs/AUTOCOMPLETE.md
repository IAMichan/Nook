# Autocomplete Anywhere

While you type in any macOS app, Nook suggests how your sentence continues as inline gray ghost text right after your cursor—powered by a local model on your Mac. Press **Tab** to accept the next word, or **⇧Tab** to accept the entire suggestion. If you keep typing matching characters, the ghost text advances smoothly; any other key dismisses it immediately.

Enable it in **Settings › Autocomplete**, which has five tabs:

- **General:** On/off toggle and live status (including what stands in the way: Secure Input held by another app, named when macOS says which, and macOS's own inline predictions, with a button to Keyboard settings), model selection (your Brain model, a local GGUF model on your Mac, a custom `.gguf` file, or a one-click download), suggestion length and eagerness, and customizable shortcuts.
- **Apps:** Every installed app with its icon and toggle. Search or filter by Enabled/Disabled. Click any app for custom per-app instructions, whether to leave `Tab` to the app, mid-line suggestions, learning, font size, vertical offset, or pausing. Below the app list, configure per-website rules and instructions inside browsers. Terminals, code editors, and password managers are disabled by default. VS Code and its forks (Cursor, Windsurf, VSCodium) are the exception: suggestions appear in their chat inputs (Claude Code, Copilot Chat) and other prose fields, never in the code editor or the terminal, which `CompletionFieldKind` recognizes from the web classes Electron exposes (`AXDOMClassList`: Monaco, xterm.js).
- **Style & Context:** What context the model receives (active app and window title, Nook memory, screen text, clipboard), your writing style and local learning, emoji shortcodes, typo correction, and alternative word suggestions.
- **Playground:** A text box to try autocomplete without leaving Settings. It uses the same model, accept keys, and suggestion settings as other apps (your instructions and memory included; screen, window, and clipboard are left out), and shows the model's raw answer, what was shown, how long it took, and the full prompt the model read. Nothing typed there is saved or counted in the statistics.
- **Statistics:** Usage counts for today, this week, and all time, a 30-day chart, estimated typing time saved, characters completed, acceptance rate, daily streak, and top apps. Nook only records aggregate counts—never what you typed.

## Key Features

- Inline ghost text in any app. **Tab** accepts one word, **⇧Tab** accepts the full completion, or just keep typing. All shortcuts are customizable.
- 100% local inference on your Mac: uses either your built-in MLX brain model or a GGUF model via `llama.cpp` (Metal-accelerated). No word limits and no subscription.
- Custom instructions globally, per app, and per website.
- Optional context from your Nook memory, active screen text (e.g., the email you are replying to), and clipboard.
- Finishes the word you are typing (token healing, with both the MLX and the GGUF engine) and keeps suggesting while you edit a sentence: before its period or a closing bracket always, and mid-line (on by default, shown as a card under the cursor so it never covers your text). An emoji picker (type `:` and pick by arrow keys, number or click, or type on to search: `:smile`, `:duim`), inline typo fixes, and numbered word alternatives.
- Optionally, the number of words completed today next to Nook's icon in the menu bar.
- Learns from your own writing locally while automatically filtering out secrets (`CompletionPrivacy`): card numbers, IBANs, national ID numbers, API keys and tokens, private keys, passwords after a label in any of Nook's five languages or in a setting named after one (`DATABASE_PASSWORD=…`), bearer tokens, and logins and signatures in links. The same filter cleans screen text and the clipboard before they reach the model. The writing samples and turned-down phrases are encrypted on disk (AES-GCM, `CompletionSealing`) with a key in Nook's Keychain vault; files from earlier versions are sealed at the next launch. When the Keychain does not give the key, the sealed files are left as they are and nothing is written over them. Besides the words you use, it learns the phrases you write again and again (`CompletionPhrases`: which word follows the one to three words before it, recent writing weighing more). A phrase you nearly always finish the same way is suggested at once, without waiting for the model; pressing Escape on one makes it count less. The most frequent phrases also go into the prompt.
- Respects macOS smart quotes. On battery (and in Low Power Mode) it pauses by default, since every suggestion is a model run on this Mac. Under **On battery** in the General tab you can let it run lighter instead: the model only runs once you pause typing and writes nothing ahead, suggestions are shorter, a smaller model on this Mac can take over, or suggestions come only with the **Suggest now** key.

## How It Works

1. **Keys: a listening tap and hot keys, as in Cotypist.** A `CGEventTap` that only listens (`AutocompleteKeyTap`, `.listenOnly`, key downs and clicks) tells the controller what was typed; macOS hands it a copy and never waits for it, so a busy Nook can never slow down typing in another app. The keys autocomplete takes from the app are system hot keys (`AutocompleteHotKeys`, Carbon `RegisterEventHotKey`), registered only while they mean something (`CompletionKeyGate.hotKeys`): Tab, Shift-Tab and Escape while a suggestion or typo fix shows; the numbers and Escape for other words; the arrows, numbers, accept keys and (after two letters) Return for the emoji picker; and the shortcuts that are set. With nothing on screen Tab is the app's, as in Cotypist: there is no waiting for a suggestion on its way. A hot key that turns out not to be autocomplete's (the state changed on the way, a number key that types no number on this layout) is posted on to the app, with the hot keys away for a moment so it does not come back. macOS refuses some modifier sets as hot keys (such as ⌥ alone); such a key is logged and then works as a plain key in the app. Windows has the same behaviour through its low-level hook, which decides on its own thread and is armed only while something is on screen.
2. **Reading the focused field (`AutocompleteField`).** Right after the key (no pause by default; the model already starts on the text the key predicts), Nook reads the focused text field via the macOS Accessibility API: text before and after the cursor, selection, cursor bounds, placeholder, window title, and browser URL. For Chromium, Electron, and WebKit apps, it uses text markers and `AXManualAccessibility` / `AXEnhancedUserInterface`. Password fields (`AXSecureTextField`) and Secure Event Input mode are strictly ignored.
3. **Choosing the completion source.** Typing `:` at the start of a word opens the emoji picker; a misspelled preceding word offers a typo correction; otherwise, the local language model generates a continuation.
4. **Prompt construction (`PromptBuilder`).** Assembles a structured prompt containing your instructions and Nook memory, writing samples, screen context, clipboard, target app/site metadata, and the text preceding your cursor, as two parts: the context (fixed per field) and the text (`CompletionPrompt`). The prompt window slides in 500–1,000 character increments so the model's KV cache is reused across keystrokes.
   - **Base models** read it as a document: the context as a header, then your text.
   - **Chat (instruction-tuned) models** read it in their own chat template (`CompletionChat`): the context and the task ("continue the text I am typing, in its language and style") go in the user's turn, and the model's answer starts with the text you typed, which it simply goes on with. Fed a bare document, a chat model tends to answer the text or drift into the headers instead. This applies to the brain's MLX models and to any GGUF model with a chat template that is not a base model. Text that reads as a control token (`<|im_end|>`, `<end_of_turn>`) is taken out of the context, so screen text cannot end the user's turn.
5. **Model inference (`LlamaCompleter` / `BrainCompleter`).** Runs `llama.cpp` (via `LlamaSwift`) or `mlx-swift-lm` on Metal. A certainty threshold filters out low-confidence starts and stops generation as soon as token confidence drops. Words you frequently write receive a small logit bonus. With `llama.cpp`, the suggestion starting with the likeliest word and the ones starting with the next two likeliest are written side by side in one batch, and the surest one per token is shown (see *Best of three* below).
6. **Rendering & typing (`GhostOverlay` & `AutocompleteTyper`).** Draws the suggestion in a click-through, non-activating overlay panel matching the host app's font, size and text colour (`AXAttributedStringForRange`: `AXFont`, `AXForegroundColor`). The suggestion is a translucent shade of the colour of the text at the cursor, so it fits a dark editor or a coloured link; apps that report a wrong colour can get the system grey (per app: *Grey suggestions*). Accepted words are injected as Unicode key events so your clipboard is never touched.

## Speed

- **Whole suggestions:** a suggestion shows once the model is done with it, never word by word while it is still being written (the engines can stream, but a suggestion that grows under the cursor is harder to read than one that waits a few milliseconds).
- **Spelling tables at load:** the tables token healing spells unusual words from (every token's bytes, by their first bytes) are built right after the model loads, not on the first name you type.
- **Fixed context per field:** everything before your text in the prompt is gathered once per field, so a key only costs the last few tokens.
- **Writing ahead:** while a suggestion is on screen, Nook already writes how the text goes on after it. Taking the last word shows that next suggestion at once, moved past the word just typed, before the app has even drawn it (`settle()` then lines it up with the real cursor); typing it to the end shows it as soon as the app has the text. A suggestion taken while it is still being written starts the writing ahead right away, from what is on screen.
- **Chains do not stall:** every suggestion ends where the model got unsure, so the next one starts at an unsure point. Suggestions right after one that was taken whole use lower thresholds (`CompletionOptions.continuing()`), so Tab, Tab, Tab keeps going instead of stopping after two or three suggestions.
- **Window text without blocking:** with "What is on your screen" on (the default), the text of the window you write in comes from Nook's screen reading when that is on, and otherwise autocomplete reads the focused window itself through Accessibility (`AppTextReader.focusedWindowText`), once per field and off the main thread. The first suggestion in a field does not wait for it.

## Quality

- **Token healing (GGUF):** with the cursor in the middle of a word, the prompt is read without that word, and the model has to spell it again from the tokens that fit it (`CompletionHealing`). A cut-off word like "morg" tokenizes unlike anything the model learned from. While you type the rest of the word, the prompt stays the same, so it is faster too.
- **Best of three (GGUF):** greedy decoding picks each word on its own, and a sentence that starts with the second likeliest word is sometimes much surer as a whole. After a space, the suggestion that starts with the likeliest token and the two that start with the next likeliest ones (each at least 3% likely) are written side by side, one batch per token, sharing the prompt in the cache: on the GPU a batch of three costs about what one token does. Another candidate replaces the greedy one only when it clears the same bar for its first word, says at least as much, and is clearly surer per token (`CompletionRanking`: a mean log-probability at least 0.3 higher). The candidates not shown go into the memo: typing the start of one shows it at once. Mid-word (token healing) and on battery without branches there is one greedy suggestion. This holds for both engines.
  - **One design for every engine:** the controller asks for a number of candidates (`CompletionOptions.candidates`, 3); each engine says how many it can write in one batch (`CompletionEngine.candidateLimit`) and writes that many. Every engine measures a suggestion with the same rules (`CompletionCandidate`: when it stops, how sure it is per token, whether it ended mid-word), so `CompletionRanking` compares them the same way whichever engine wrote them.
  - **The brain's MLX model ranks too:** its candidates are rows of one batch. The prompt is read once, its cache repeated per row, and every step reads one token per row (`[rows, 1]`), all at the same position. Measured with Llama 3.2 3B Instruct (4-bit) on this Mac: 17.7 ms per step for one row, 18.3 ms for three, and row 0 writes exactly what the single-sequence greedy path writes. Only caches known to take rows this way are batched (plain and rotating key-value caches); a model with another kind (quantized, chunked, the state of hybrid models such as Qwen 3.5) writes the greedy row alone.
- **Sure of the whole suggestion:** besides the threshold per token, the product of all token probabilities must stay above a threshold (`minSequenceProbability`), so a suggestion ends where it starts to drift. "Eagerness" sets all thresholds.
- **No half words:** when the model stops because it is no longer sure, it stops at the last whole word.
- **No copies:** three words or more that already stand in the last lines before the cursor are not shown.
- **Screen text without the interface:** only lines that read as text go into the prompt (four words or more, or a short sentence such as "Kom je morgen?"), not buttons, labels and menus.
- **The text you reply to:** autocomplete reads the pane around the field (`AppTextReader.textAroundFocus`: the nearest ancestor of the focused element with enough text, such as the conversation above a message field) instead of the whole window with its sidebar first. The prompt keeps the *end* of it (`CompletionScreenText.recent`), so the newest messages sit right above your text. Nook's own screen reading fills in until that read is done.
- **Fresh context:** the context around a field is gathered again after a message is sent (the field empties) and every three minutes, so new incoming messages are seen. The clipboard only goes into the prompt for five minutes after it was copied. Clipboard contents marked as concealed (password managers), transient or auto-generated (`org.nspasteboard.*`) never go in.
- **Typo check:** the last finished word is checked with the macOS spell checker, also while the next word is being typed (the fix then retypes that word's start too). Short or mixed-language text is checked in the likely languages and the ones set in macOS, and only counts as a typo when none of them knows the word. The fix comes from the autocorrect choice or the spell checker's guesses, but only when it is one or two letters away (`CompletionTextAnalysis.isLikelyFix`). Words you write often, and fixes turned down with Escape, are left alone.
- **No suggestion on a typo being typed:** while the word before the cursor (four letters or more, no capitals inside) is not a word in any of your languages, nor the start of one (`NSSpellChecker.completions(forPartialWordRange:)`), no suggestion comes: the model would only finish the mistake. On by default, under **Typo correction** (`holdOnTypo`, `AutocompleteTypos.suspectsWordBeingTyped`).
- **British or American:** a suggestion follows the English spelling you write (`CompletionSpelling`): "colour" next to "favourite", "color" next to "favorite". Decided per kind of difference (-ise/-ize, -our/-or, -re/-er, doubled l, single words such as grey/gray), from the field first and your writing samples second; "-ize" is only decided by its own words, since Oxford spelling writes it next to "colour". Pairs whose American form is another British word (cheque/check, programme/program) are left out. A word the model finishes ("colo" + "ur") is judged whole and left as you started it.
- **Coding agents in terminals:** terminals stay off for the shell, but suggestions come in the prompt of a coding agent running in Terminal or iTerm2 (Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, OpenCode…), as in Cotypist (`CompletionTerminal.agentPrompt`). The input line must start with `>`, `›` or `❯` and the agent's frame must be around it (a rule or box line above and below, or one of its footer hints such as `? for shortcuts` or `context left`), so a heredoc's `> ` never counts. Only the text typed after the marker goes to the model, without the box, status lines or placeholder; the window title (which agents animate) is left out of the field's identity. When the terminal gives no font, the ghost text takes the one in its own settings (iTerm2's default profile, Ghostty's `font-family`, kitty's `font_family`). Ghostty, kitty, Alacritty, WezTerm and Warp do not give their text or cursor through Accessibility yet. Off with **In terminals: in a coding agent's prompt**; a terminal turned on in the Apps tab gets suggestions in the shell too.
- **How text goes in, per app:** typed as key events (the default; one character at a time once an app is seen to drop the rest), one character at a time from the start, with every space a press of the space bar (for apps that drop a space typed as a character), or pasted with ⌘V for apps that mangle typed text. A paste marks the clipboard as transient (clipboard managers skip it) and puts back what was there 0.4 s later, unless something else was copied meanwhile.
- **Force-activate:** the **Suggest now** key also works where suggestions are off (an app, a site, a small field), as Cotypist's force-activate does: suggestions then stay on in that field until the focus moves to another field or app (`AutocompleteController.forcedApp`). Never in a password manager or under Secure Input.
- **LoRA adapter:** a GGUF LoRA adapter made for the GGUF model can be chosen under the model; it is applied at full strength (`llama_set_adapters_lora`), and one that does not fit the model is skipped. The brain's MLX model takes none.
- **macOS predictions:** when macOS's own inline predictions are on, Settings offers to turn them off right there (the same user setting as Keyboard › Text Input), not only to open Keyboard settings.
- **Recorded texts:** the writing Nook learned from can be read under **Style & Context** (the newest 100, with the app and date) and deleted one by one, besides all at once.
- **Where a suggestion came from:** each one logs its source and time (Console, subsystem `nl.imdigital.nook`, category `autocomplete`, "served from": memo, learned phrase, written ahead, model, or model (cold) when it still had to load).
- **Chat models first:** automatic model choice prefers a chat (instruct) model, then a base model, and a code or math model last. Measured with Gemma 4 E4B on eight everyday messages (Dutch and English, with and without screen context): the instruct model made a suggestion for all eight, sure of its first word 36–98% of the time; the base model of the same size made one for only two, mostly too unsure (6–28%) to show anything. Settings recommends Gemma 4 E4B Instruct from 24 GB of memory and E2B Instruct from 16 GB.

## Emoji Picker

Typing `:` at the start of a word opens a picker under the cursor: the emoji you used last, then the most used. Typing on (`:lach`, `:heart`) filters it at once, without reading the field again. Pick with the arrow keys and Tab (or Return, once two letters are typed, so `:D` and Return still sends), with 1–9 for the row with the selection, or with a click: while the picker shows, its panel takes clicks (so a click on it stays out of the app), and the listening tap hit-tests the click against the card. Escape, a space or punctuation closes it.

A preferred gender for people (🤷, 🙋, 🏃 and the like as a woman or a man) and, with a skin tone chosen, each emoji in the default yellow next to it are set under the skin tone.

Text emoticons offer their emoji too (**Emoji for emoticons**): after `:-)`, `;)`, `<3` or `^_^` at the start of a word, a card shows 🙂, 😉, ❤️ or 😊 and the accept key puts it in place of the emoticon; typing on keeps the emoticon. `:D`, `:P` and `:O` are names to the picker already, which then shows their face first.

## Model Selection

In **Automatic** mode, Nook selects a model in this order:

1. **An existing GGUF model already on your Mac.** `CompletionModelLocator` scans `~/Library/Application Support`, `~/Library/Caches`, LM Studio's models folder, and Hugging Face / `llama.cpp` caches (skipping vision projectors, embedding models, and files under 50 MB) without copying or modifying the file.
2. **A GGUF model downloaded in Settings.** Under **Settings › Autocomplete › General › Download GGUF models**, you can download recommended base models or the Gemma 4 E2B/E4B instruct (chat) models with live progress, or paste any Hugging Face `.gguf` URL.
3. **Your local Brain model.** Reuses Nook's built-in MLX model (`BrainCompleter`) with KV-cache checkpointing so no extra model download is needed. Whenever the chat brain is actively generating a reply, autocomplete yields automatically.

## Windows (Tauri)

The Windows build (`apps/tauri`) has a first version of Autocomplete Anywhere. It is compiled for Windows in CI but has
not been run on a real Windows PC yet.

- **Logic:** the platform-free parts are ported to `apps/tauri/core/src/services/autocomplete/` with unit tests:
  `suggestion` (whole words, no copies, typing through, accept word/all), `prompt` (context per field, sliding window,
  chat templates with the answer begun, control-token scrub), `privacy`, `prefs` (per-app and per-site rules; terminals,
  code editors and password managers such as `WindowsTerminal.exe`, `cmd.exe`, `Code.exe`, `1Password.exe`,
  `KeePass*.exe`, `Bitwarden.exe` are off by default), `stats` (counts only), `session` and `emoji`.
- **Model:** no llama.cpp. Suggestions come from the local server the brain already uses: Ollama (`/api/generate` with
  `raw: true`, greedy, a small `num_predict`, `keep_alive`, the same `num_ctx` as the brain, streamed and cancelled by
  the next key) or LM Studio (`/v1/completions`). When the server returns `logprobs`, the Mac's certainty thresholds
  apply (`ConfidenceGate`); otherwise only the token limit and the stops end a suggestion. The external brain is used
  only when chosen in Settings › Autocomplete after a confirmation; the default is local only.
- **Platform:** a low-level keyboard hook (`WH_KEYBOARD_LL`, plus `WH_MOUSE_LL` for clicks) on its own thread swallows
  only the accept keys, Escape and the picker's keys while something is on screen. The focused field is read with UI
  Automation (`TextPattern2.GetCaretRange`, `TextPattern`, `ValuePattern` as a fallback; the Win32 caret when UIA has
  no rectangle). Password fields, apps with more rights than Nook and the secure desktop are skipped. Accepted text is
  typed with `SendInput` and `KEYEVENTF_UNICODE`, never through the clipboard. The ghost text and the emoji card are a
  transparent, click-through, never-activated, topmost window that is excluded from capture.
- **Emoji picker:** as on the Mac, including the emoji that fit the text (keywords, no model). Clicks on the card are
  taken in the mouse hook and hit-tested against the card's layout (`PickerLayout`). Emoticons (`:-)`, `<3`) open
  the same card with their one emoji (`Picker::for_emoticon`), which the accept key puts in place of the emoticon.
- **British or American spelling:** as on the Mac (`spelling.rs`, the same table), from the field only, since
  Windows does not learn from your writing yet. The preferred gender for people in emoji is the same as on the Mac
  (`render_person`); the yellow variant next to toned emoji is not ported yet.
- **Not yet ported:** screen and window text as context, learning from your writing and the word bonus, token
  healing, writing ahead and the suggestion memo, ranking several candidates, the app's text colour for the ghost
  text, typo fixes and holding back on a typo being typed (no spell checker yet), word alternatives, coding agent
  prompts in terminals (Windows Terminal is read through UI Automation, not yet tried), insert modes per app (typing
  stays `SendInput`), force-activate (Windows has no Suggest now key yet), LoRA adapters (Windows runs a local server
  such as Ollama, which loads its own), the macOS predictions switch (Windows has none), the list of recorded texts
  (nothing is recorded yet), per-website rules (the browser's address is not read), battery behavior, the
  menu-bar count and the 30-day chart.

## Building & Testing

The `LlamaSwift` package ([mattt/llama.swift](https://github.com/mattt/llama.swift), pinned to `2.10549.0` / `llama.cpp` `b10549`) is configured in `apps/macos/project.yml`. Run `xcodegen` in `apps/macos` after pulling changes.

Foundation-only autocomplete logic is tested in `apps/macos/Tests/NookTests/AutocompleteTests.swift` (`GGUFReader`, `CompletionModels`, `CompletionSuggestion`, `CompletionPreferences`, `CompletionEmoji`, `CompletionPrivacy`, and `CompletionLearning`) and runs via `./scripts/core-tests.sh --filter AutocompleteTests`.
